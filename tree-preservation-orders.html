Tree Preservation Orders in Calderdale: what you need to know before any work.

Cutting down or heavily pruning a protected tree without permission is a criminal offence - fines can reach £20,000 per tree. The good news: checking is free, and most genuine work is approved.

What is a TPO?

A Tree Preservation Order is made by the local planning authority - for Brighouse, that's Calderdale Council - to protect trees that add to the local area. A TPO can cover a single tree, a group, or every tree within a defined area. If your tree is protected, you need written consent before felling, topping, lopping or uprooting it.

Check first

Calderdale Council can confirm whether a tree is covered by a TPO, and whether your address sits inside a conservation area - where different rules apply to any tree over 7.5cm thick.

Apply in writing

Work on a protected tree needs an application to the council describing the tree, the work and the reasons. The council usually decides within eight weeks.

Dead or dangerous

Dead trees and trees that pose an immediate risk can usually be dealt with without full consent - but you should still notify the council and keep evidence, such as photographs.

Conservation areas: the six-week rule

Parts of Brighouse and nearby villages fall inside conservation areas. There, you must give the council six weeks' written notice before working on most trees, even without a TPO. A tree surgeon who works locally will know the boundaries and handle the notice as part of the job.

What a good tree surgeon does for you

Reputable local tree surgeons check protection status before quoting, prepare the application or notice with the right technical wording, and carry out approved work to British Standard BS3998. If anyone offers to "just take it down" without mentioning a check, that is your warning sign.
